My all time favorite verse in scripture is:

> Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy and my burden is light.
> — Matthew 11:28-30 (NIV)

I thought I understood it. I was wrong, or at least incomplete.

Something found me that I had no control over. And for the first time in a long life of doing the right things and landing well, I had no lever to pull.

That broke something open.

It drove me back to another teaching I thought I understood:

> Truly I tell you, unless you change and become like little children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven. Therefore, whoever takes the lowly position of this child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven.
> — Matthew 18:3-4 (NIV)

I had always read this as a call to innocence or simplicity — to quiet the analytical mind. I now understand it differently. The faith of a child is not naive. It is the faith of someone who has no means of providing for or protecting themselves. It comes from a place of genuine vulnerability and complete dependence. That is the posture. Not chosen innocence. **Honest powerlessness**.

I have lived a life of doing the right things and, without fully realizing it, quietly believed that my good outcomes — and avoided bad ones — were partly a result of my own steering. I accepted grace for the good fortune but took private credit for staying out of trouble. It was a facade of control I had built without knowing I was building it.

I have also questioned my faith many times. The existence of God. Whether a God who personally cares about my fears and burdens is even coherent. I resolved this a long time ago by arguing against myself: a God who can be unmasked as fake by honest questioning is not much of a God. So I gave myself permission to follow my analytical mind wherever it led. I always returned to faith. The teachings of Jesus resonated. And I repeatedly experienced a peace I could not explain away.

But this time was different. The surrender was complete — no quiet backstage negotiation about whether things would turn out okay, no illusion of control over outcomes. Just surrender, to whatever comes. The peace arrived before anything was resolved. While the situation remained unchanged. That is what the verse actually means. Not comfort after the storm. Rest in the middle of it, at the point of surrender — not at the point of solution.

I do not fully know whether I have accepted the feared outcome or whether somewhere I still hope it resolves well. I question my own thinking about that. But something deeper is settled. And if it does resolve well, I already sense I will know it was not my doing. That knowing, in advance, is new.

Control was always more of an illusion than I admitted. Grace was always doing more of the work — in the good outcomes and in the protection from bad ones. Accepting that honestly, not just intellectually, is what faith actually requires.

I am being drawn closer and deeper into faith than I have ever been. The thing that found me and threatened to undo me may be the thing that finally got me there.

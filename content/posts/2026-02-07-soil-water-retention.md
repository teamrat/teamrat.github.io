---
title: "On Measuring What Matters in Soil Water Retention"
date: 2026-02-07T09:00:00-08:00
tags: ["soil-physics", "research"]
---

We've gotten very good at measuring soil water retention curves. Pressure plates, tensiometers, TDR—the instrumentation keeps improving. But I'm increasingly convinced we're measuring the wrong thing, or at least asking the wrong questions.

<!--more-->

A retention curve tells you how much water soil can hold at a given matric potential. That's useful. But what really matters for plants, for carbon cycling, for everything we care about, is water accessibility over time. A soil that releases water slowly is fundamentally different from one that releases it quickly, even if their retention curves look identical.

We need better ways to think about dynamic behavior, not just static equilibrium.

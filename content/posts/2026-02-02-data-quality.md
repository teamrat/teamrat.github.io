---
title: "Data Quality Over Data Quantity"
date: 2026-02-02T14:00:00-08:00
tags: ["research", "data"]
---

Working with a collaborator who has fifteen years of soil moisture data. Sounds amazing, right? Except the sensors were never calibrated, site metadata is incomplete, and nobody remembers exactly where half the sensors were installed.

<!--more-->

Fifteen years of unusable data is worse than one year of careful measurements. At least with one year, you know what you don't have. With fifteen years of bad data, you can convince yourself you have answers when you really have noise.

This isn't a critique of the collaborator—they were doing the best they could with the resources available. It's a critique of how we fund research. We reward deployment and data collection. We don't reward calibration, documentation, and maintenance. Then we wonder why so many datasets are unusable five years later.
